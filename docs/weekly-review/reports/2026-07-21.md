## Findings

**F1 — Direction is the dominant effect.**
NO: 39 markets, 88.6% hit at avg ask 0.753 → **+0.133/contract [+0.110, +0.156]**.
YES: 31 markets, 33.6% hit at avg ask 0.523 → **−0.187/contract [−0.225, −0.147]**.
Both CIs exclude zero. Not one lucky cell: crossed with edge band, all three NO
bands at n≥6 are positive (+0.157, +0.091, +0.048) and all three YES bands at
n≥9 are negative (−0.147, −0.271, −0.170) — 62 of 70 markets agreeing. The two
dissenters are `>40` bands at n=2 and n=6.

*Counter-evidence:* live YES P&L by week runs −1.02, −9.06, **+1.54, +1.18**.
The two most recent weeks went the opposite way. Those are ~10-position samples
at ~1 contract and cannot overturn 70 markets, but they are the only real-money
evidence and they disagree.

**F2 — The LLM's probability estimate is worse than the Poisson baseline it is
handed.** Parsed from each signal's stored prompt, so exactly point-in-time
(n=61 markets, `in_market_count=0`):

| estimator | mean P(yes) | Brier |
|---|---|---|
| Poisson, 30d rate | 0.455 | **0.2093** |
| model | 0.536 | 0.2407 |
| constant base rate | 0.377 | 0.2349 |
| Poisson, 365d rate | 0.575 | 0.2517 |

The model barely beats a constant. The failure is asymmetric: where it says YES
(n=34) it estimates **0.859 against a 52.9% truth**, scoring *worse* than the
base rate (0.3155 vs 0.2491); where it says NO (n=27) it is the best arm
(0.1464). Trading the 30d Poisson directly with the same gates returns **+0.24
vs the model's −2.30**, and on NO-only signals the model is the *only* losing
estimator (−0.63, against +0.91 for 365d and +0.33 for 30d).

Confidence does not rescue it. `corr(confidence, hit) = +0.168`, and
Kelly-by-confidence does improve the model (−24.2% → −20.6% per unit staked, and
full Kelly at conf=1.0 is worse at −23.8%, so shrinking toward the market when
unsure is doing real work). But confidence rises with the **ask** (0.635 → 0.746
→ 0.730), so the model is surest where the market is surest — profit edge by
confidence band is −0.075, +0.004, −0.030. The ranking is unchanged under all
three stake weightings.

**F3 — The live exit policy beats every alternative tested.**
`force_exit:algo_exit` saved **+$13.23 [+0.114, +1.895]** over 13 live exits,
12 helped / 1 hurt. Signal exits saved +$2.27 over 19 (CI spans zero). The
candle-based sweep — real price paths, **74/74 live positions covered**, no
censoring, no survivor conditioning — puts actual **−$4.23** ahead of every
stoploss threshold (−$7.46 to −$18.23 worse) *and* ahead of no-stoploss by
**$16.57**.

The pooled view says the opposite: 232/430 covered, overwhelmingly paper, and
removing the stoploss entirely scores **+$95.01**. All 185 of those stoplosses
are paper. This is the clearest case in the report for never pooling the modes.

**F4 — The stoploss has been disabled since 2026-06-04.** `stoploss=-1.0` is
unreachable on a 0–1 price scale (commit `7ac225b`); the last stoploss exit in
the whole dataset is 2026-06-03, the day before. So F3 is a recommendation not
to *re-enable* it, not an endorsement of its tuning.

**F5 — Entry gates are not costing trades; the limit-entry rule already does
that job.** `order_types.entry="limit"` posts at `estimated_probability −
min_edge` with a 2h timeout — not a market order at the ask. Replayed through
the real rules over stored price paths:

| gate | blocked n | fill rate | $/contract |
|---|---|---|---|
| `min_edge` | 32 | **12%** | +0.008 [−0.061, +0.069] |
| `max_edge` | 8 | 100% | −0.271 [−0.514, −0.031] * |
| `max_spread` | 2 | 100% | −0.505 [−0.540, −0.470] * |
| `min_confidence` | 3 | 100% | +0.197 [−0.350, +0.570] |

Mechanical once stated: the limit sits below the ask *precisely when edge <
min_edge*, so **28 of 32 blocked signals would never have traded.** `min_edge`
and the limit rule express the same constraint. `max_edge` and `max_spread` are
blocking real losers.

The uncomfortable half: **admitted signals lose money** — `min_edge` admitted is
−0.210/contract [−0.369, −0.057] at a 100% fill rate.

**F6 — No setting of any *existing* gate is profitable, because direction is not
a gate.** Replayed sweeps across all seven numeric gates put every cell negative,
so every apparent "gain" is loss avoided by trading less; the limiting case is
trading nothing, at $0. Tightening `max_days_to_close` to 1.0 looks best at
−0.34 because it keeps 3 of 36 markets.

That is a statement about the gate *set*, not about the strategy.
`StrategyConfig` has no `directions` field, so every sweep averages over YES and
NO where +0.133 and −0.187 cancel. Adding the missing filter:

| config | markets | $/signal | total |
|---|---|---|---|
| all current gates, as shipped | 36 | −0.106 | **−$3.83** |
| all current gates **+ NO only** | 12 | +0.068 | **+$0.82** |
| all current gates + YES only | 24 | −0.194 | −$4.65 |

One pre-specified change flips the book from −$3.83 to **+$0.82**.

**F7 — Tightening a gate defers entry rather than skipping the market, and the
deferred price is measurable.** The sweep reports it directly:

| `max_days_to_close` | markets | deferred | avg entry px | total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7.0 (current) | 36 | 0 | 0.696 | −3.83 |
| 5.0 | 32 | **19** | 0.665 | −3.41 |
| 3.0 | 20 | **8** | 0.638 | −0.56 |
| 1.0 | 3 | **3** | 0.447 | −0.34 |

Of the 24 markets whose first eligible signal was >3 days out, tightening to 3
**keeps 8** — entered on a later signal, priced off the candle path at that
signal's own time. Entry price moves with the gate (0.696 → 0.447), which is why
this needed price paths rather than a single snapshot.

**F8 — The 19.5% neutral-fallback rate is by design.** 248 of 263 `price_moved`
neutral assessments have no LLM call, versus 3 of 73 `scheduled`. That is the
documented carry-forward path for clones whose evidence set is unchanged.
Recorded so a future review does not re-raise it as an outage.

## Recommendations

### R1 — Restrict entries to NO on KXTRUMPSAY

Add a `directions: list[str]` field to `StrategyConfig` (default `["YES","NO"]`,
so no existing strategy changes) and set `["NO"]` for `PoliticsEdgeStrategy`.
~10 lines plus a test.

- **Evidence**: F1 and F6. 70 markets, both direction CIs excluding zero,
  consistent across every edge band with n≥6. Live: 64 YES entries netted
  −$7.36, 33 NO entries +$3.46.
- **Expected effect**: replaying the shipped config over signal-v11's 36 markets
  gives −$3.83; NO-only gives **+$0.82** — a **+$4.65 swing** over ~2.3 weeks,
  so roughly **+$2/week at one contract per trade**.
- **Smaller than F1 implies.** F1's +0.133 is a buy-and-hold figure. Replayed,
  NO *alone* is −0.028 at a 23% fill rate and only turns positive once the
  existing gates apply. The mechanism is "NO **and** the current filters".
- **Risk**: cuts volume ~60%, slowing every future measurement including this
  one. The last two live weeks had YES profitable, so a regime effect would cost
  us those. All-NO also concentrates one payoff shape — high-priced favourites
  (avg ask 0.84 hitting 94–100%), exactly the profile that takes a large loss
  when a favourite fails.
- **Confidence**: **medium**. Signal-level evidence strong; live evidence thin
  and currently disagreeing. Raised by two more reviews with the YES CI still
  excluding zero.
- **Verification**: `by direction` in next week's section 5, plus live P&L split
  by direction for the window.
- **Revert trigger**: the cumulative YES edge CI includes zero in any later
  review, **or** declined YES signals would have made money three weeks running.

### R2 — Trial the Poisson baseline as the probability estimate

Not adoptable here — it is a signal change and goes through the benchmark gate
in `freqpred/signal/CLAUDE.md`. Candidate arm: `min(p_model, poisson_30d)`,
confidence untouched. The cap can only reduce YES overconfidence, never increase
it.

- **Evidence**: F2. Model Brier 0.2407 vs 0.2093; on NO-only the model is the
  only losing estimator; trading the 30d baseline returns +0.24 vs −2.30.
- **Expected effect**: roughly +2.5 per-contract-units over 61 markets on this
  sample. Unlike R1 it scales with size rather than being capped by direction.
- **Risk**: in-sample, n=61, one series, one prompt version. The best lookback
  window is unstable across subsets (365d wins on NO, 7d on YES) — **do not tune
  the window on this data.** A benchmark run costs API dollars and may be null.
- **Confidence**: **medium** that the model's estimate is beatable; **low** on
  which specific baseline.
- **Verification**: `scripts/benchmark_signals.py` with a frozen scenario bank
  and `--training-cutoff`; the paired Brier delta CI is the gate, not this table.
- **Revert trigger**: n/a — an investigation. Drop if the paired delta spans zero.

### R3 — Change nothing about exits or the stoploss

An explicit do-nothing, because the pooled data argues loudly for the opposite.

- **Evidence**: F3, F4.
- **Expected effect**: $0 by construction. The value is not chasing ~$158 of
  paper-driven "improvement", or the ~$95 the pooled candle sweep credits to
  *removing* the stoploss.
- **Risk**: rests on 13 live algo exits. If volume grows and stoplosses start
  firing, this has no live evidence behind it.
- **Confidence**: **high** for current conditions, **low** as volume scales.
- **Verification**: a `stoploss` row appearing at all in a live-scoped review.
- **Revert trigger**: five or more live stoploss exits in a window, or a live
  stoploss row with negative `saved` whose CI excludes zero.

## Overfitting check

A 324-cell grid over direction × min_edge × max_edge × min_confidence ×
max_days_to_close found better in-sample configurations — best being **NO, edge
in [0.20, 0.40]** at +0.155/signal, +$1.86 over 12 markets. Those are *not*
recommendations: 324 draws over 57 markets selects hard on noise, and nothing
here corrects for it.

R1 differs in kind. The direction split was identified independently on 70
markets with a CI excluding zero *before* any grid was run — one pre-specified
hypothesis, not the argmax of a search.

  effect, and it points at signal quality, which is R2's territory.
